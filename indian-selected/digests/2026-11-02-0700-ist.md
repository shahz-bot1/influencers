### 1. Rahul Jain — "How to Spot GREAT Stocks, Avoid Bad Stocks and Survive Market Cycles - Rahul Jain G2G" (1 Nov 2026; collab: Good 2 Great By Rahul Jain and 2 more)
Rahul Jain interviews fund manager and author Ajay Sharma about his book "Good to Great to Gone": every great company eventually stops being great, so investing is as much about knowing when to avoid or exit as when to buy. Sharma's first rule is to avoid the large loss: cap any position at 7% at cost / 10% at market price, and never buy just because a stock has fallen 50–80%. He sorts businesses into Brahma (creation), Vishnu (dominant stability) and Mahesh (decline) phases, and reads industry structure as a lake with fish — profits depend on lake size and fish count. Asian Paints is the worked case: a 30-year whale at a 70–80 multiple whose dominance broke when Birla Opus entered with ₹10,000 crore, lifting capacity ~50% against ~7% demand growth. HDFC Bank is in a first-ever "exile" of FII selling plus a leadership vacuum — cheap, but it may stay cheap. His sell discipline is a thesis-by-price matrix plus an expected-return test: 15% growth for 10 years with a multiple decaying from 100 to 25 merely breaks even, so exit when expected return falls below the index plus a risk premium (5% generally, ~20% for micro/small caps). Other rules: use median, not average, multiples; treat promoter selling as a red flag to investigate, not an automatic sell; and apply an integrity filter that walks away from governance doubts.
No explicit calls in this one. Companies appear only as case studies, and Sharma states twice that he is not giving recommendations: Adani Ports praised as a near-monopoly lake, Jio/Airtel as a telecom duopoly, Zomato/Swiggy as a distribution moat, and Anthropic (his Feb 2024 investment via a friend's Menlo Ventures allocation) presented explicitly as luck, not a repeatable process.
